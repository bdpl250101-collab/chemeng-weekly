  • Unseen but Avoidable: How Impurities Bias Electrocatalysis from Interface to Interpretation
    ACS Catalysis 2026-11-07
    Hongmin An, Jongsu Noh, Jinyeop Kim 외 5명
    초록 보기

    Impurities exert profound effects on electrocatalytic measurements and interpretations. Trace levels of contaminants introduced through electrolytes, electrodes, gases, or cell materials routinely generate false activity signals, accelerate degradation, and alter product selectivity. This review categorizes impurity effects by functional impact, including misleading activity, compromised stability, and shifted selectivity, and offers strategies to recognize, isolate, and mitigate these influences. We argue that reliable and reproducible electrocatalytic data require an impurity-aware approach, integrating both experimental protocols and systems engineering. By intentional design, impurities can be transformed from hidden liabilities into controlled variables, ensuring that reported advances in electrocatalysis reflect intrinsic catalyst properties and translate into durable energy technologies.

  • Cation-Effect-Driven Modulation of Interfacial Solvation Structures Enables Fast-Charging Lithium-Ion Batteries
    Journal of the American Chemical Society 2026-11-07
    Shuibin Tu, Dixing Ni, Shijie Xu 외 3명
    초록 보기

    Fast battery charging is limited by sluggish Li+ transport at the anode/electrolyte interface, which is governed by the interfacial solvation structure (ISS). However, negative polarization disfavors anion accumulation at the anode interface, hindering the formation of anion-rich ISS required for rapid Li+ transfer. Here, leveraging the cation effect in electrocatalysis, we propose a cation-mediated strategy to reshape the interfacial electric field and construct anion-rich ISS in commercial electrolytes. With benzyltriethylammonium serving as a model cationic regulator, we show that it accumulates near the polarized graphite surface and forms a cation layer that alters the local electric field. This cation-mediated interfacial regulation promotes PF6– accumulation at the interface, redirects early solid electrolyte interphase (SEI) formation toward anion-derived pathways, mitigates solvent decomposition, and accelerates Li+ desolvation and transport. As a result, graphite-based pouch cells deliver ∼88.2% state of charge within 6 min, maintain stable cycling for 2000 cycles with ∼71% capacity retention, and provide ∼218.2 Wh kg–1 with 10 min charging. Beyond LiPF6-based electrolytes, this cation effect also promotes reduction of the anions in other lithium-salt systems. This work bridges electrocatalytic cation effects and battery interfacial chemistry, offering a practical route to fast-charging lithium-ion batteries.

  • Lattice Nitrogen Couples Cu δ+ Stabilization With Pathway Control for CO 2 ‐to‐Methanol Electroreduction
    Angewandte Chemie International Edition 2026-11-06
    Chengli Rong, Justin Prabowo, Jian Peng 외 5명
    초록 보기

    Dynamic catalyst reconstruction is an unavoidable feature of Cu‐catalyzed CO 2 electroreduction, yet for methanol synthesis it creates a fundamental stability–selectivity conflict: the cathodic potentials required for CO 2 reduction simultaneously destroy the partially oxidized Cu sites needed to retain oxygenated intermediates. Here, we show that lattice‐incorporated nitrogen resolves this conflict by regulating both catalyst evolution and reaction‐pathway selection in amorphous CuO. Nitrogen creates a resilient Cu–N/O coordination environment that suppresses excessive Cu reduction and preserves catalytically relevant Cu δ+ sites. The optimized N/a‐CuO catalyst achieves a methanol Faradaic efficiency of 48% in an H‐type cell and a methanol partial current density of 42.8 mA cm −2 in a flow cell, corresponding to 266.3 µmol h −1 cm −2 . Operando spectroscopy reveals enhanced accumulation of bridge‐bound *CO and its subsequent conversion to oxygenated intermediates. Density functional theory further shows that nitrogen‐induced charge redistribution stabilizes Cu δ+ sites and selectively lowers the free energy of the pathway‐diverging *CHOH intermediate through a favorable C–N interaction, promoting C─O bond retention and methanol formation over methane. These findings establish lattice nitrogen as a strategy for coupling dynamic active‐site stabilization with intermediate‐specific pathway control in multielectron electrocatalysis.

    The intrinsic electronic structure of Ru‐based catalysts exhibits strong adsorption of oxygen‐containing intermediates, which runs counter to the adsorption requirements of the oxygen reduction reaction (ORR), limiting their ability to achieve high activity.     초록 보기

    Predicting enantioselectivity in asymmetric (organo)catalysis remains a longstanding challenge, particularly in systems where stereocontrol is governed by weak, highly flexible, and substrate-dependent noncovalent interactions. We introduce a previously unexplored chiral imidazolium/aryloxide betaine organocatalyst platform for asymmetric 1,3-dipolar cycloadditions of CH-acidic imines with maleimides that operates with exceptional efficiency, reaching turnover numbers of up to 3000 while maintaining high enantiocontrol.
