    ACS Catalysis 2026-10-31
    Yingying Fan, Ruijie Yang, Zheng Li 외 3명
    초록 보기

    Solar-driven conversion of N2 and H2O into ammonia under ambient conditions offers an alternative to the energy-intensive Haber–Bosch process, yet current advances are largely limited by material-focused strategies that insufficiently address coupled transport and interfacial reaction processes. Herein, we report a sub-nanofluidic photochemical membrane with catalytically active channels that integrate light harvesting, catalytic activation, and mass transport within a unified architecture for solar ammonia synthesis. The membrane consists of ZnIn2S4 atomically thin sheets and molybdenum disulfide (MoS2) quantum monolayers, forming ordered sub-nanometer channels that are intrinsically catalytic. This design establishes a confined reaction environment in which nanofluidic transport, gas–liquid interfacial regulation, and charge carrier dynamics are synergistically coupled. In particular, the combination of hydrophilic–hydrophobic interfaces and sub-nanofluidic-engineered serpentine flow pathways enhances reactant accessibility, residence time, and photon utilization, thereby overcoming diffusion and activation limitations typical of conventional photocatalytic systems. With these merits, the membrane achieves an ammonia production rate of 4.39 μg cm–2 h–1 (2.48 mmol gcat.–1 h–1) with ∼70% ammonia selectivity and maintains stable operation over 48 h of continuous testing. The achieved ammonia production rate compares favorably with those of reported photocatalytic platforms and some electrocatalytic N2 fixation systems. This work introduces a catalytically active nanofluidic channel paradigm for solar-driven N2 fixation and highlights the importance of material-transport co-engineering in bridging the gap between nanoscale catalytic design and device-level performance for chemical synthesis.

  • Microscopic Origins of Metal‐Dependent pH Effects in Hydrogen Evolution: Metal–Water Interactions Dictate Proton Transfer Pathways
    Angewandte Chemie International Edition 2026-10-29 OER / HER
    Wanyi Wang, Zhuoyang Xie, Renqin Yu 외 6명
    초록 보기

    The hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) is a prototypical proton‐coupled electron transfer (PCET) reaction, yet the atomistic origin of its metal‐dependent pH effect remains unclear. Here, combining constant‐potential ab initio molecular dynamics with in situ infrared spectroscopy, we unravel the microscopic origins of metal‐dependent pH effects in HER catalysis on Pt(111) and Au(111). We reveal that HER kinetics are governed by two competing spatial proton transfer topologies associated with the Volmer water‐dissociation step: a low‐barrier cross‐layer proton transfer (CLPT) pathway and a high‐barrier intra‐layer proton transfer (ILPT) pathway. For Pt(111), strong metal–water interactions yield a pH‐responsive water network. Under acidic conditions, a balanced intra‐ and cross‐layer hydrogen‐bond network promotes the CLPT in the Volmer step, thereby accelerating HER kinetics. By contrast, alkaline media induce a rigid H‐down interfacial water layer that favors the sluggish ILPT pathway. Conversely, weak metal–water interactions on Au(111) maintain a pH‐insensitive, predominantly parallel‐flat water orientation that restricts the Volmer step to cooperate only with the high‐barrier ILPT in both acidic and alkaline solutions. Our findings establish a fundamental causal link among metal–water interactions, interfacial hydrogen‐bonding network, proton transfer pathways, and HER kinetics, providing atomistic insight into the design of pH‐robust electrocatalysts.
