    Advanced Energy Materials 2026-09-27 OER / HER
    Yung‐Hung Huang, Tsung‐Hsin Liu, Ya‐Wen Tang 외 11명
    초록 보기

    Seawater‐based artificial leaves are fundamentally constrained by anodic surface poisoning and insufficient photovoltage for bias‐free reaction under complex electrolytes. Here we report a pH‐gradient photoelectrochemical–electrochemical (PEC–EC) system that couples photoelectrochemical hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) and electrochemical ethylene glycol oxidation reaction (EGOR) enabled by CO‐tolerant 1T‐PtTe 2 in both fresh water and natural seawater. As an anodic electrocatalyst for EGOR, platinum ditelluride exhibits a low onset potential of 0.42 V vs. RHE, achieves highly selective production of the value‐added product glycolate (GA) with a Faradaic efficiency of 96.4% at 0.9 V vs. RHE, and maintains high stability in natural seawater. The demonstration of this PEC–EC system provides a general framework for designing bias‐free photoelectrochemical devices operating at appreciable current densities and advances the practical implementation of seawater‐based artificial leaves.

  • What Drives the Selectivity of 5-(Hydroxymethyl)furfural Electroreduction over Copper in Mildly Basic Medium?
    ACS Catalysis 2026-09-26
    Fabio Loprete, Giovanni Malagoli, Adriano Sacco 외 5명
    초록 보기

    2,5-Bishydroxymethylfuran (BHMF) is an important bio-based monomer that can be produced via the electrochemical reduction of 5-(hydroxymethyl)furfural (HMF) on Cu electrocatalysts. Under mildly alkaline conditions, however, BHMF formation competes with hydrodimerization to 5,5′-bis(hydroxymethyl)hydrofuroin (BHH) and oligomerization, particularly at HMF concentrations relevant for scale-up. Here, we combine electrochemical experiments and grand-canonical density functional theory (GC-DFT) to elucidate the origin of these competing pathways during HMF electroreduction on Cu-based foam catalysts at pH 9.2. Our results suggest that BHH formation and oligomerization arise from two distinct mechanisms: hydrodimerization might proceed via surface-catalyzed C–C coupling between adsorbed HMF species, whereas oligomerization is associated with the desorption of a ketyl intermediate into the electrochemical double layer. In contrast, BHMF formation preferentially occurs through the hydrogenation of an alkoxyl intermediate. The relative contribution of these pathways is modulated by catalyst morphology and the electroactive Cu surface area. Increasing the surface area through Cu nanoparticle electrodeposition enhances BHMF selectivity from 40% to 58% at 50 mM HMF and significantly suppresses oligomerization at –0.51 V vs RHE. Overall, our results highlight the importance of balancing HMF surface availability and H* availability and provide a mechanistic framework linking catalyst morphology and electrochemical conditions to control the selective HMF electroreduction on Cu and offer design principles for maximizing BHMF yields while minimizing hydrodimerization and carbon loss.

  • Nonmonotonic Charging, Overscreening, and Ion Competition in the Electric Double Layer of Platinum Quantified by X-ray Spectroscopy
    Journal of the American Chemical Society 2026-09-26
    Alfred Larsson, Dogukan Yilmaz, Jan Sebastian Dominic Rodriguez 외 8명
    초록 보기

    The electric double layer (EDL) is an integral part of the local reaction environment in electrocatalysis. Indeed, the strong electric fields and modified interfacial water structure accompanying ion accumulation in the EDL have been shown to impact the performance of nearly every electrocatalytic reaction. However, quantifying interfacial ion accumulation experimentally is a long-standing challenge, leaving the atomic-level picture of electrolyte effects in electrocatalysis incomplete. Here, we address this challenge using an interface-sensitive in situ X-ray fluorescence spectroscopy approach based on a mesoporous Pt electrode to track interfacial ion concentration at the Pt-electrolyte interface in acidic media. For nonadsorbing anions, we show that the concentration-dependent surface free charge follows classical Gouy–Chapman theory. At negatively charged surfaces, cations and protons compete to screen the surface free charge in the EDL, with a strong dependence on ion valency.
