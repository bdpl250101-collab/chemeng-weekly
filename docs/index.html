  • Predicting CO2 Reduction Selectivity on Large-Scale Cu–Pd Nanoparticles via a Multi-Scale Machine Learning Framework
    ACS Catalysis 2026-11-13 OER / HER
    Wooseok Lee, Dong Hyeon Mok, Hyun Dong Jung 외 2명
    초록 보기

    The conventional computational approach to electrocatalyst evaluation has long been limited by its reliance on simplified slab models and adsorption energies on bare surfaces, which fail to capture the structural complexity of realistic nanoparticles (NPs) and the presence of adsorbates that decorates electrode surfaces under working conditions. To bridge this gap, we present a multi-scale computational framework that combines machine learning potential (MLP)-driven molecular dynamics (MD) simulations with the explicit realization of potential-dependent surface coverage and high-throughput evaluation of active sites. Applying this framework to the CO2 reduction reaction (CO2RR) on Cu–Pd alloys, we generated realistic 8 nm NP morphologies using a SevenNet MLP trained from scratch and determined the coverage states and binding energies of key intermediates (CO*, H*, and OH*) across all surface sites using a zero-shot universal MLP. Our coverage-resolved evaluation reveals a systematic transformation of product selectivity across the composition–potential space, from formate production on Cu-rich NPs to CO evolution and the competing hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) on their Pd-rich counterparts. Notably, a discernible C1+ fraction emerges on the Cu3Pd alloy at intermediate overpotentials, reflecting the promotional effect of dilute Pd incorporation. This work establishes a methodology that bridges the gap between atomistic predictions and the structural and electrochemical complexity of working nanocatalysts, paving the way for more accurate in-silico discovery of electrocatalysts.

  • Secondary Sphere Modification Tunes C–OH Bond Cleavage and Redistributes Rate Control in Electrocatalytic CO2 Reduction by Molecular Cr Complexes
    ACS Catalysis 2026-11-14
    Megan E. Moberg, Luke A. Flaxman, Diane A. Dickie 외 1명
    초록 보기

    Mitigating the contribution of anthropogenic carbon dioxide (CO2) emissions to global climate change has motivated the development of earth-abundant transition-metal catalysts capable of electrochemically reducing CO2 to value-added chemical products. It has previously been demonstrated that bipyridine-based CrN2O2 complexes can selectively reduce CO2 to carbon monoxide (CO) under electrochemical conditions. To improve the kinetic parameters (turnover frequency) of this catalyst family, the effect of adding –OMe pendent substituents to the bipyridine backbone of the ligand structure was explored. Here, two catalysts bearing different numbers and positions of –OMe pendent groups are reported and analyzed with respect to their impact on the proton-transfer step that accompanies C–OH bond cleavage as a part of the catalytic cycle for CO2 reduction. Combining the observed isotope effect for each catalyst with an intrinsic value computed for that catalyst resolves the contributions of CO2 binding and C–OH bond cleavage to rate control individually, giving varying degrees of rate control for CO2 binding across the series. Separating the underlying rate constants shows that, across the series, the rate constant for C–OH cleavage varies by about two orders of magnitude, whereas that for CO2 binding varies by less than a factor of two. This work suggests that the addition of –OMe groups can provide a kinetic enhancement compared to complexes without the substituents; however, the benefit is tempered by shared rate control with the CO2 binding step of the reaction, which was previously shown to contribute to the observed rate.

  • Directional Nitrite Spillover Couples to Interfacial Hydrogen-Bond Networks for Ampere-Level Nitrate-to-Ammonia Electrosynthesis
    Journal of the American Chemical Society 2026-11-14
    Qingshuang Xu, Kangyu Lou, Zihan Liu 외 5명
    초록 보기

    Electrocatalytic nitrate reduction to ammonia is often constrained by the proton-coupled hydrogenation of adsorbed nitrite (*NO2), a step commonly accelerated through hydrogen spillover and local reactive-hydrogen (*H) supply. This hydrogen-centered view, however, treats *NO2 largely as a passive intermediate and overlooks whether its migration can reconstruct the interfacial hydrogen-bond-network environment required for downstream hydrogenation. Here, we show that directional *NO2 spillover couples site-specific intermediate transport with interfacial hydrogen-bond-network reorganization. In a Cu–Ag dual-site model interface, nitrate activation and *NO2 formation occur preferentially on the Cu sites, whereas migrated *NO2 species are stabilized and hydrogenated on the Ag-associated sites. In situ spectroscopy, kinetic isotope measurements, distribution-of-relaxation-time analysis, and theoretical simulations reveal that this Cu-to-Ag relay enriches K+-associated interfacial water, reinforces hydrogen-bond connectivity, and facilitates *H delivery for *NO2 hydrogenation. The optimized CuAg N-doped carbon (CuAg–N-C) achieves near-unity Faradaic efficiency (FE) for NH3 with a yield rate of 411.8 mmol g–1 h–1 and retains 83% FE at 1 A cm–2 in a flow cell. The catalyst further retains substantial nitrate-to-ammonia recovery in simulated and authentic industrial water matrices. More broadly, this work establishes *NO2 spillover as a dynamic interfacial mechanism linking intermediate transport, water-network organization, and proton-coupled nitrogen conversion, providing a framework for sustaining molecular-scale selectivity under chemically complex reaction environments.

  • Magnetic‐Field Modulation of Oxygen Evolution at Structurally Constrained Single‐Atom Sites With a Secondary Photoresponse
    Angewandte Chemie International Edition 2026-11-13 OER / HER
    Jin‐Hua Liu, Yu‐Ze Sun, Zheng Wei 외 7명
    초록 보기

    External physical fields have emerged as promising tools for dynamically regulating electrocatalytic reactions, yet how different stimuli influence catalysis within structurally constrained active sites remains poorly understood. We establish structurally constrained Ni single‐atom‐anchored CdS as a model platform to investigate magnetic‐field modulation of the oxygen evolution reaction (OER) and its relationship to the photoresponse. Operando electrochemical measurements, quasi in situ spectroscopic characterizations, magnetic analyses, and density functional theory calculations reveal fundamentally distinct response pathways. The photo‐induced response is strongly potential‐dependent, with a clear photocurrent response at lower bias, but little additional current under strongly anodic OER conditions. In contrast, magnetic fields regulate the electronic structure of Ni active sites by modifying Ni 3d–S 3p orbital coupling and spin configurations, thereby reducing the free‐energy requirement for the potential‐determining *OH‐to‐*O conversion step without inducing significant changes in the coordination environment. Notably, the catalytic and spin responses observed under magneto‐optical conditions closely resemble those induced by magnetic fields alone, indicating that magnetic‐field‐driven spin regulation dominates OER kinetics in structurally constrained single‐atom active sites. This work demonstrates that different external fields access distinct electronic degrees of freedom and establishes a conceptual framework for external‐field‐regulated electrocatalysis beyond conventional structure‐based catalyst design.
