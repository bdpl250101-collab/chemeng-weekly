  • Quantum Chemical Insight into Species Competition in Electrochemical Upgrading of Bio-Oil
    ACS Catalysis 2026-10-25
    Christina M. Wark Kessler, Keyvan Malaie, Jannelle Casanova-Rañoa 외 5명
    초록 보기

    Bio-oil derived from lignocellulosic biomass represents an alternative to fossil fuels as an energy source and chemical feedstock. Fast pyrolysis of lignocellulosic biomass yields a rich mixture of oxygenated organic species with low energy density and low stability, which can be electrochemically upgraded to increase energy density and stability and produce value-added chemicals. Here, we focus on electrocatalytic hydrotreatment of furfural (FF) and 4-propylphenol (4-PP), two constituents of raw bio-oil. In a binary mixture, the presence of FF has been shown to inhibit the reduction of 4-PP. The underlying mechanisms of these competing reactions are probed using density functional theory (DFT) and experimental electrochemical deposition techniques. The binding energies of FF and 4-PP, as well as their respective reduction products and associated reaction thermodynamics, are examined comparatively on multiple facets of single-crystal platinum and ruthenium catalysts. FF is shown to bind competitively compared to 4-PP, particularly on Pt(111) and Ru(0001), and hydrogenation products such as tetrahydrofurfuryl alcohol (THFA) and 4-propylcyclohexanol (4-PCOL) were found to bind less strongly to the surfaces than the reactants. FF reduction side products, including dimers furoin (FUI) and hydrofuroin (HF), were also found to bind competitively with 4-PP. No thermodynamic barriers were observed for FF hydrogenation to THFA, whereas 4-PCOL formation is favored on Pt facets, and propylcyclohexane (PC) formation is favored on Ru. The presence of barriers to 4-PP hydrogenation indicates that FF hydrogenation is thermodynamically favored on Pt and Ru. Further, limiting potential calculations suggest that the FF reduction pathways are generally favorable around 0 V vs. RHE. In contrast, the 4-PP reduction pathways require a more cathodic bias, achieved at potentials less than -0.1 V vs. RHE. The adsorption behavior of FF-derived reduction products on polycrystalline Pt is experimentally validated using a Quartz Crystal Microbalance (QCM). Such experiments yield estimates of aqueous-phase free energies of adsorption, which are compared with computational results that are correlated to the aqueous phase. Overall, the DFT model results suggest that the observed inhibition of 4-PP by FF is due to competitive adsorption and favorable FF reaction thermodynamics.

  • Bridging Spectroscopy and Reactivity: Identifying the True Reactive CO Species in Electrocatalytic CO Oxidation
    Journal of the American Chemical Society 2026-10-25
    Tian-Yi Yang, Ya-Jun Huang, Hao-Fei Geng 외 7명
    초록 보기

    In situ electrochemical vibrational spectroscopy has revolutionized our understanding of electrocatalysis. However, establishing a rigorous correlation between spectroscopic signatures and reaction kinetics is still very challenging because high-coverage surface species may dominate the signal, whereas the truly reactive participants may be short-lived or spectroscopically silent. To disentangle spectators from true reaction participants, we developed a high-sensitivity spectroelectrochemical platform that integrates the rotating disk electrode with surface-enhanced Raman spectroscopy (RDE-SERS). This approach enables the simultaneous acquisition of reaction kinetics and interfacial molecular information under well-defined mass transport conditions. Using this versatile approach, we investigated the benchmark carbon monoxide oxidation reaction (COOR) on the Pt surface, which enabled decoupling between the spectroscopically observable species and the catalytically active species. Despite persistent and intense Raman signatures of adsorbed CO (COad) in the diffusion-limited region, COad predominantly acts as a spectator that blocks surface sites without effectively participating in the reaction, as confirmed by the isotope substitution experiments. Conversely, the oxidation current is sustained by the continuous flux of solution-phase CO (COsol), which is identified as the kinetically relevant reactant. These findings offer a new perspective on the roles of adsorbed species versus solution-phase species during dynamic electrochemical reactions. Collectively, the RDE-SERS methodology is established as a reliable strategy for identifying true reaction pathways, bridging molecular-level spectroscopic identification with macroscopic reactivity in complex electrocatalytic systems.

  • Understanding the Instability Origin for Design of Robust Pure RuO2 Anode Catalyst in PEM Water Electrolysis
    Journal of the American Chemical Society 2026-10-24 OER / HER
    Lin-Lin Wang, Xuan-Xuan Cheng, Xiao-Long Zhang 외 7명
    초록 보기

    Ruthenium dioxide (RuO2) shows great promise as an acidic oxygen evolution reaction (OER) catalyst in proton exchange membrane water electrolysis (PEMWE), yet its insufficient stability─often attributed to Ru overoxidation─actually has a hidden cause. Herein, we show that the true culprit is instead residual chloride impurities inherited from the most commonly used RuCl3 precursor. Owing to strong Ru–Cl coordination, substantial chlorine residues are trapped in the RuO2 lattice during conventional synthesis; during electrolysis, they gradually leach out, dramatically accelerating Ru dissolution and lattice oxygen loss and triggering structural collapse. Guided by this insight, we developed a small-molecule-assisted synthesis that yields Cl-free RuO2. The resulting catalyst preserves exceptional structural integrity, sustains its initial activity through 80,000 accelerated degradation cycles, and delivers stable PEMWE operation at 500 mA cm–2 for 500 h, outperforming all reported single RuO2 catalysts and rivaling even the best doped or composite Ru-based catalysts. Our findings resolve a long-standing paradox in RuO2 catalysis and establish chloride elimination as a critical design principle for durable oxide electrocatalysts.

  • Interfacial Water‐Networks Regulate the Anodic Stability of Carbon Electrodes

    초록 보기

    Carbon‐based materials are widely used in modern electrochemical technologies, yet their susceptibility to oxidation at positive electrode potentials poses a durability challenge. While this degradation has commonly been addressed through modification of the carbon material itself, the interfacial chemistry that activates carbon oxidation remains poorly understood. Here, we show that the hydrogen‐bonding (HB) network of interfacial water strongly influences anodic carbon stability. Operando differential electrochemical mass spectrometry combined with in situ attenuated total reflection surface‐enhanced infrared absorption spectroscopy reveals that corrosion accelerates as the interfacial HB network becomes increasingly disrupted, implicating weakly H‐bonded water as a reactive water population associated with carbon oxidation. This work highlights interfacial water‐network modulation as a promising design principle for extending the anodic stability of carbon‐based electrodes.

    Proton exchange membrane water electrolyzers (PEMWEs) are central to renewable‐powered green hydrogen production. As the benchmark electrocatalyst for the oxygen evolution reaction (OER), conventional single‐phase IrO 2 is constrained by linear scaling relations among oxygenated intermediates, compounded by severe mass‐transport limitations and poorly regulated interfacial water structures.     Tao Yang, Boning Wu, Qi Sun 외 2명
    초록 보기

    Understanding electrocatalysis requires direct knowledge of how charge carriers are distributed and consumed at active sites under the operating conditions. However, the spatiotemporal dynamics of injected electrons in working catalysts remain largely inaccessible, hindering a mechanistic understanding of catalytic heterogeneity. Here, we develop an operando time-resolved spectroelectrochemical microscopy that enables direct and quantitative mapping of electron density with microsecond temporal resolution and micrometer spatial resolution.
