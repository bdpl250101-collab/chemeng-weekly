  • Engineering Advanced Electrocatalysts for Energy‐Saving Zinc−Air Hybrid Batteries: Concurrent Energy Storage Coupled With Chemical Upgrading
    Advanced Energy Materials 2026-10-18
    Xian‐Wei Lv, Tao Meng, Mengyue Zhang 외 5명
    초록 보기

    Additive‐assisted zinc−air hybrid batteries (ZAHBs) that couple favorable additive oxidation with oxygen reduction represent ideal energy storage candidates, distinguished by high theoretical energy density, reduced charging voltage, and functionality for electrochemical upgrading of organics or wastewater. Herein, a critical overview of ZAHBs is provided, commencing with the fundamental electrochemistry (including advantages, working mechanisms, cell configurations, and evaluation parameters) and the selection criteria for small‐molecule additives (including theoretical potential, solubility, stability, toxicity, and volatility). We then summarize recent progress in three categories of ZAHBs (i.e., wastewater‐purified, value‐added, and redox‐mediated), discussing alternative anodic oxidation reactions (e.g., alcohol, urea, hydrazine, and iodine oxidation) coupled with cathodic oxygen reduction, along with the underlying catalytic mechanisms. Particular emphasis is placed on catalyst design strategies spanning electronic (ligand engineering, heteroatom doping, electron delocalization, orbital hybridization, and built‐in electric field), geometric (defect engineering, curvature engineering, and nano‐confinement effect), and interfacial (high‐energy interface, heterostructure, interfacial microenvironment, and core–shell structure) modulations. Finally, we discuss the remaining challenges and future perspectives for advancing ZAHBs. This review offers valuable guidance for the rational design and practical deployment of next‐generation energy devices.

  • Oxygen‐Coordinated Nickel Single Atoms Couple Photohole Capture With C–H Cleavage in Photoelectrocatalytic Glycerol‐to‐Dihydroxyacetone Conversion
    Angewandte Chemie International Edition 2026-10-17 OER / HER
    Rufeng Tian, Lu Niu, Jian Wang 외 4명
    초록 보기

    Selective photoelectrochemical oxidation of glycerol to dihydroxyacetone (DHA) requires photogenerated holes to reach the substrate faster than they drive oxygen evolution, followed by C–H cleavage without C–C fragmentation. Here, oxygen‐coordinated Ni single atoms on the TiO 2 (001) nanosheet arrays regulate both steps. Isopropanol‐probe and in situ glycerol spectra support Ni‐promoted dissociative adsorption through the secondary hydroxyl group. Radical spectroscopy and interfacial kinetics support subsequent oxidation through glycerol‐derived alkoxy species, accompanied by a 15–20‐fold acceleration of glycerol‐coupled hole transfer and suppressed oxygen evolution. Product‐based glycerol isotope effects supported the kinetic involvement of central C–H cleavage in DHA formation, while calculations showed that Ni coordination increased the energetic preference for C–H over C–C cleavage from 0.05 to 0.38 eV. Operando spectroscopy and weaker DHA adsorption support retention of the three‐carbon skeleton and product release. Under charge‐normalized conditions, the Ni single‐atom photoanode achieved a DHA Faradaic efficiency of approximately 66.6%, compared with 22.2% for unmodified TiO 2 and 16.4% for the NiO x control. DHA‐selective charge utilization persisted in recirculating‐flow operation with 0.5 M glycerol. These results support coupled control over substrate adsorption and subsequent bond selectivity as a design principle for selective PEC polyol oxidation.

  • Adsorption Energies Are Necessary but Not Sufficient to Identify Good Catalysts
    ACS Catalysis 2026-08-24 OER / HER
    Shahana Chatterjee, Alexander Davis, Lena Podina 외 7명
    초록 보기

    As a core technology for industrial chemical synthesis and electrochemical energy storage, electrocatalysis is central to decarbonization strategies aimed at combating climate change. In this context, computational and machine learning driven catalyst discovery has emerged as a major research focus. These approaches frequently use the thermodynamic overpotential, calculated from adsorption free energies of reaction intermediates, as a key parameter in their analysis. In this paper, we explore the large-scale applicability of such overpotential estimates for identifying good catalyst candidates by using datasets from the Open Catalyst Project (OC20 and OC22).     Cong-Hui Xu, Yu-Chao Song, Jing-Jing Zhang 외 2명
    초록 보기
