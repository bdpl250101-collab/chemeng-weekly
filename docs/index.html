  • Mitigating Surface Poisoning by Bidirectional Regulation for Efficient C1‐C2 Molecule Electrooxidation
    Angewandte Chemie International Edition 2026-10-03
    Shuping Liang, Yipin Lv, Guozhu Chen 외 5명
    초록 보기

    The rational design of Pt‐based electrocatalysts to alleviate CO intermediates poisoning during electrooxidation of C1‐C2 fuels (methanol, ethanol, formic acid) presents a grand challenge. Current detoxification strategies predominantly focus on enhancing OH* formation to accelerate CO* oxidation. However, the periodic voltage/current oscillations induced by accumulated OH species are often overlooked. Such accumulation of secondary poisoning intermediates inevitably leads to a high overpotential and poor operational stability. Herein, guided by machine learning screening, we resolve this dilemma by designing ultrathin PtPdGaRuFeMo high‐entropy alloy nanosheets featuring Ga and Ru dual‐promoter sites for the bidirectional regulation of CO* and OH* adsorption. Both theoretical analysis and experimental results demonstrate that the synergistic Ga and Ru sites modulate the electronic structure of active centers, balancing the dynamic adsorption of CO* and OH* to mitigate surface poisoning. Consequently, the as‐prepared catalysts deliver excellent specific activities of 5.4 mA cm −2 for methanol and 3.5 mA cm −2 for formic acid electrooxidation, surpassing commercial Pt/C by factors of 9.7 and 12.8, respectively. This study establishes a dual‐promoter bidirectional regulation, providing a promising route offering a robust solution to the stability‐activity trade‐off in fuel‐cell electrocatalysts.

  • The Chameleon Role of Interfacial Hydroxyls: Reaction-Condition-Dependent Dynamics in Methanol Electro-Oxidation
    ACS Catalysis 2026-10-02
    Hongkai Bu, Yang-Gang Wang
    초록 보기

    The electrocatalytic oxidation of methanol on platinum represents a model system for alcohol fuel-cell chemistry, yet the precise role of interfacial hydroxyl (OH*), a key oxidative species, remains contentious and is often viewed simplistically as either a co-reactant or a poison. Herein, ab initio molecular dynamics simulations with explicit solvation were employed to transcend this binary perspective, revealing OH* as a dynamic interfacial mediator whose coverage influences methanol activation and mechanistic pathways. Under oxidative potentials, increasing OH* coverage leads to the formation of an interfacial adsorbate layer interconnected by hydrogen bonds. Frequent proton transfer within this layer facilitates spontaneous O–H bond cleavage of methanol, while fluctuations in the hydrogen-bond network further promote its pre-oxidation and activation. In contrast, OH*-mediated C–H oxidation involves a high free-energy barrier, and increasing OH* coverage reduces the availability of Pt sites required for C–H dissociation, potentially contributing to surface poisoning. Importantly, site competition induced by high OH* coverage also alters the configuration of the reaction-generated HCHO intermediate, reducing the accessibility of further C–H cleavage toward CO* while enabling direct reaction with OH* toward formate through a CO*-free pathway. Together, these results reveal a coverage-dependent trade-off among O–H activation, the availability of Pt sites for C–H bond cleavage, and subsequent pathway selection. Regulating interfacial OH* coverage therefore provides a possible means to tune methanol oxidation activity and pathway selectivity.
