  • Curvature-Induced Electric-Field Effects Govern Controllable N2 Electrocatalysis on Single-Atom Catalysts
    ACS Catalysis 2026-10-10
    Nana Hu, Xingshuai Lv, Thomas Frauenheim 외 2명
    초록 보기

    Electric field-enhanced electrocatalysis represents an innovative strategy for improving the efficiency of electrochemical transformations. However, quantitative evaluation of microscopic electric-field effects under operando conditions remains challenging, limiting a rigorous, predictive understanding of field-mediated reaction regulation. Here, by integrating rigorous numerical methods, constant-potential microkinetic simulations, and machine learning models, we establish electric-field intensity as a quantitative descriptor that directly correlates local fields with catalytic performance. Using curvature-engineered M–N–C single-atom catalysts as a model platform, we demonstrate that curvature-induced electric fields modulate electron occupation at active sites and shift the potential of zero charge in a controllable manner. This field-driven electronic perturbation selectively tunes adsorbate binding energetics, thereby dictating limiting potentials, steering reaction pathways, and governing the thermodynamics and kinetics of interfacial proton-coupled electron transfer in nitrogen electroreduction. Notably, we uncover an inverted volcano–type dependence of catalytic activity on curvature, wherein optimal activity is achieved within a moderate field-strength window. This nonmonotonic trend underscores the necessity of balanced curvature regulation, rather than simply maximizing field intensity, in optimizing electrocatalytic performance. These findings provide a quantitative framework for monitoring and regulating catalytic trends under realistic operating conditions via curvature-induced electric fields, offering generalizable design principles for diverse SAC systems and electrochemical reactions.

    Nitrate electroreduction offers a pathway for coupling wastewater remediation with sustainable ammonia production, yet its mechanistic details under operating conditions remain poorly understood.     초록 보기

    Electrocatalytic nitrate reduction is a key reaction in nitrogen cycling and water remediation, yet the mechanistic interpretation of its voltammetric response remains poorly understood. Here, we combine cyclic voltammetry analysis with in situ product detection using scanning electrochemical microscopy (SECM), and hydrodynamic analysis using rotating disk electrode (RDE), to investigate nitrate-to-nitrite electroreduction on silver.
